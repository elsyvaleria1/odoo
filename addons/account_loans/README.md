# Loans Management (`account_loans`)

Records a loan's amortization schedule and books the journal entries it implies.

## The two models

`account.loan` is the agreement: the amount borrowed, the total interest, the three accounts
and the journal the entries are written to. `account.loan.line` is one row of the amortization
schedule — a date, a principal and an interest amount. Everything else on both models is
derived from those.

A schedule arrives one of three ways: imported from a CSV/XLS/XLSX file, generated by the
compute wizard, or typed into the inline list. Nothing constrains the interval between rows,
so quarterly and annual schedules are as valid as monthly ones.

## What confirming books, per line

| Entry | Date | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Principal & Interest | month end | long term (principal), expense (interest) | short term (payment) |
| Reclassification LT → ST | month end | long term | short term |
| its reversal | the next day | short term | long term |

The reclassification moves the principal **falling due within twelve months of that line** from
the non-current to the current liability account, and reverses it the following day so the
position is stated once per period rather than accumulating. The window is a **date** window,
not "the next twelve rows" — those coincide only for a monthly schedule.

Every entry is created with `auto_post = "at_date"`; confirming posts the ones already due and
leaves the rest to the auto-post cron.

`skip_until_date` suppresses the entries for lines up to and including that date, for a loan
whose early periods were already booked by hand.

## State

`_STATE_TRANSITIONS` is the map and `_set_state()` is the only writer — no other code assigns
`state`. `_settle_state()` derives the state from what is actually posted, and
`account.move._post_entries` calls it rather than deciding for itself, so a loan closes at the
moment its last payment entry posts, whichever path posted it.

```
draft ──confirm──▶ running ──last entry posts / close wizard──▶ closed
  │                   │                                            │
  └───────────── cancel / set to draft ◀────────────────────────────┘
```

## Two guards worth knowing about

**Deleting a loan.** `account.move.generating_loan_line_id` is `ondelete="set null"`, not
`cascade`: a database-level cascade would delete journal entries in SQL, below `unlink()`, the
audit trail and the hash chain. `_unlink_except_posted_entries` refuses to delete a running or
closed loan, or one linked to posted entries, and disposes of the rest through
`_unlink_or_reverse()` so audit-protected entries survive as cancelled. This mirrors
`account_depreciation._unlink_if_model_or_draft`.

**Replacing a schedule.** `_clear_schedule()` refuses a non-draft loan. Both entry points — the
file upload and the compute wizard — go through it, so neither can silently discard posted
entries.

## The schedule generator

`lib/amortization.py` is ours; its day-count conventions derive from
[pyloan](https://github.com/sudo-dakix/pyloan) (MIT). It returns `datetime.date` and `Decimal`,
raises `ScheduleError` rather than printing, keeps the annual rate at full precision, and gives
the closing payment the residual so the principals always sum to the amount borrowed and the
balance ends at zero. A zero rate degenerates to equal principal instalments. Principal is
clamped at zero, so a rate high enough to exceed the instalment produces an interest-only
period rather than a growing balance.

The compute wizard's preview is a computed non-stored one2many of
`wizard.account.loan.create.schedule.line`: the records are `NewId`, nothing is written, and what the
preview lists is what `action_save` stores.

## Permissions

Writing a loan requires `account.group_account_manager`; `account.group_account_readonly` may
read. Every header button is grouped to match — `account.group_account_invoice` is a *sibling*
of `readonly`, not a level above it, so grouping a write action on it offers the button to
roles whose click would raise `AccessError`.
`test_buttons_are_offered_only_to_groups_that_can_use_them` asserts the invariant.
